# Problem Statement

## The problem

People carry a constant, low-level stress from not knowing what their days, weeks, and months hold. Their responsibilities, promises, habits, and feelings are scattered across calendars, paper journals, to-do apps, and their own memory. Nothing connects them.

When they fall behind, the gap between the plan and reality turns into overwhelm. Things they meant to do quietly slip away: a promise made in passing, a responsibility taken on while busy, a habit they wanted to build. They usually notice when it is already too late.

Existing tools make this worse in small ways:

- **Fragmentation.** Tasks, events, habits, and journaling live in separate apps, so the user does the work of copying, syncing, and remembering.
- **Manual replanning.** Turning a monthly and weekly plan into a realistic daily plan takes effort every evening, and falls apart on busy days.
- **False emergencies.** Missed items are shown as failures right away, even when nothing is actually due yet.
- **All-or-nothing habits.** Streak-based habit trackers make one missed day feel like losing everything, so people quit.
- **Lost thoughts.** Feelings and desires written in a journal rarely become action. They stay as thoughts.

## Who has this problem

Anyone juggling many responsibilities without a clear forecast of their time: students, people in industry and corporate jobs, stay-at-home parents, freelancers, caregivers. Two groups matter most:

- **Organizers** who already have a working system but spend too much effort keeping it together.
- **Aspiring organizers** who wish they could plan intentionally but have not found a system that sticks.

This project started from the founder's own routine: a monthly and weekly calendar copied into daily plans each evening, a physical journal for emotions and thoughts, and a separate habit app. The friction between them, and the frustration of falling behind, is the problem this app exists to solve.

## The solution

A single mobile app (iOS and Android) that turns a person's time, energy, and thoughts into one calm, coherent plan.

1. **The daily plan comes first.** Opening the app shows one chronological list for today, mixing tasks, events, and habits, so the user only has to focus on one thing at a time and check it off.
2. **The app does the planning work.** It turns the month and week into daily plans automatically, using due dates, priorities, repeating items, and free time. The user reviews and edits instead of building from scratch.
3. **Low-friction intention.** Items can be given fuzzy due dates (today, tomorrow, this week, next 2 weeks, this month) so setting intention takes almost no thought.
4. **Nothing gets lost.** One-tap capture saves a promise or responsibility in the moment. The app prompts the user later that day to turn it into a task. Missed items go into a reserve and are rescheduled into future free time, and they only demand attention when they are actually due.
5. **A forecast that shows the way out.** A morning brief, a Sunday week report, and month and week views show what is coming and where the open space is. The app warns about overbooked weeks early and suggests what to push back.
6. **Energy-aware suggestions.** A three-tap check-in on energy and motivation decides which flexible tasks are proposed right now.
7. **Forgiving habits tied to identity.** Habits use weekly targets and a consistency score instead of fragile streaks, and connect to the character traits the user wants to build.
8. **Journaling that becomes action.** Guided prompts help users name how they feel and what they want, then turn those thoughts into tasks, habits, and events.

## What success looks like

After using the app, the user feels **in control, less anxious, and has a plan for the day**. They can see a list that is mostly checked off and open space ahead on their calendar, and they can enjoy free time without guilt because they know they are on track.

## Out of scope for the first version

These are planned for later, once the core daily plan works well:

- Screen-time rewards and blocking chosen apps
- Shared habits in small groups (2 to 5 people)
- A Community tab for households and group projects
- App store launch (the first version is for testing with family and friends)
